## Problem and significance

The study examines a dual-binder treatment for low-strength kaolin clay, using lime and NaOH-activated ladle refining furnace (LRF) slag. The engineering question is whether the treatment improves strength and swelling behavior, and how those changes relate to the soil's microstructure.

## My contribution

The published author-contribution statement credits me with **data analysis and interpretation, writing, and review**. ## Approach

The team evaluated mixtures containing 3%, 5%, and 8% lime and 15%, 20%, and 25% activated slag after 28 days of curing. Compaction, unconfined compressive strength, and free-swell tests were paired with SEM–EDS examination.

## Findings

The reported optimal mixture—8% lime and 20% activated slag—reached **4,619.8 kPa UCS**, compared with **63.44 kPa** for untreated kaolin. The study linked the improvement to cementitious reaction products and a denser microstructure.

## Scope and limitations

These are laboratory results for the investigated kaolin and treatment conditions. They should not be presented as a field design recommendation or as evidence of long-term environmental performance. Field transfer, durability, and site-specific material behavior require their own evaluation.
